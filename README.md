### Non-Substitution Taxonomic Boundaries (`TREE-01`)
- Do **not** substitute *Banksia serrata* L.f. (Saw / Old Man Banksia — alternate coarsely saw-toothed leaves, deeply furrowed thick corky orange-brown bark, grey-cream spikes, massive serotinous cones), *Banksia marginata* Cav. (Silver Banksia — smaller shrub/tree, scattered narrow leaves with blunt truncated/notched apices), *Banksia ericifolia* L.f. (Heath-leaved Banksia — fine ericoid/needle leaves, orange-red spikes), *Banksia spinulosa* Sm. (Hairpin Banksia — linear leaves, dark hooked styles), or *Banksia paludosa* R.Br. (Swamp Banksia — low coastal/heath shrub).
- **Working Locality Scope:** South-Eastern Australian coastal dunes, headlands, and near-coastal sands (`0–100 m AHD` typical coastal *subsp. integrifolia*; up to `1500 m AHD` including montane *subsp. monticola*); exact site and individual occurrence specimen remain pending field resolution.

## 2. Proteaceae Anatomical Subsystems (`PO:*`)

1. **Proteoid Cluster Root System & Lignotuber (`PO:0025025`, `PO:0025524`, `PO:0025531`, `PO:0000038`, `PO:0000042`):**  
   Dense seasonal mats of proteoid (cluster) rootlets (`PO:0025025`) in upper leached coastal sands exuding carboxylates for phosphorus solubilization without mycorrhizal dependence, paired with a deep dune-anchoring taproot (`PO:0000038`), 4–8 lateral structural roots (`PO:0000042`), and a basal lignotuber (`PO:0025524`) / epicormic resprouting capacity.
2. **Gnarled Tessellated Trunk & Medullary-Rayed Wood (`PO:0025004`, `PO:0004518`, `PO:0005849`, `PO:0005848`, `PO:0005597`):**  
   Twisted, often leaning coastal wind-sheared trunk (or upright woodland bole) clad in rough grey-brown deeply tessellated/furrowed corky bark (`PO:0004518`) concealing epicormic buds, overlying vascular cambium (`PO:0005597`), pale sapwood (`PO:0005848`), and dense pinkish-red proteaceous heartwood (`PO:0005849`) with conspicuous broad medullary rays (`530 kg/m³` nominal).
3. **Sympodial Pseudowhorled Branch Architecture (`PO:0009006`, `PO:0020100`):**  
   Pseudowhorled sympodial branching nodes radiating below terminal conflorescences, governed by Shinozaki/WBE pipe-model area conservation ($r_{\text{parent}}^{2.49} = \sum r_{\text{child}}^{2.49}$), fork angles `38.0°–68.0°` (nominal `50.0°`), phyllotactic golden angle `2.399963 rad`, and asymmetric salt-wind-sheared headland crown shaping.
4. **Whorled Strongly Discolorous Foliage (`PO:0025034`, `PO:0000014`, `PO:0000032`, `PO:0005679`, `PO:0005020`):**  
   Regular whorls of **3–5 oblanceolate to narrow-obovate leaves** (`4.0–20.0 × 1.0–3.6 cm`, typically `4–10 × 1.7–2.5 cm` in *subsp. integrifolia*): adult leaves with **entire margins** vs juvenile/coppice leaves with **coarsely serrate/dentate margins**; **strongly discolorous** dark dull-green glabrous waxy adaxial upper cuticle vs **bright silvery-white felted tomentose abaxial underside** that flashes dramatically in coastal winds.
5. **Cylindrical Golden-Yellow Flower Spikes & Follicular Cones (`PO:0009046`, `PO:0009001`, `PO:0009010`):**  
   Erect cylindrical golden-yellow to pale-lemon conflorescence spikes (`7–14 cm` tall, `5–7 cm` wide) packed with paired hooked styles and perianths in orthogonal spiral parastichies (blooming Jan–Jul autumn–winter peak), maturing `8–10 months` later into persistent woody cones embedded with protruding **bivalved woody follicles** (`8–15 mm`) that open spontaneously at maturity to release paired dark winged seeds separated by a woody separator.

## 4. `InstanceState64` Ontogenetic Stages & Ecological Guild

Individual variation is parameterized deterministically via a 64-byte `InstanceState64` descriptor (`seed`, `ontogenetic_age_norm`, `height_scale [0.35..1.25]`, `site_vigor [0.40..1.20]`, `environmental_stress [salt_wind_shear_bias, drought_stress, fire_char_height, epicormic_sprout_factor]`):

- **`0.00–0.15` Coastal Seedling (`0.2–1.5 m`):** Proteoid cluster root mat establishment in coastal dune sands and serrate-dentate juvenile leaf whorls.
- **`0.15–0.40` Serrate Sapling (`1.5–5.0 m`):** Developing tessellated bark, basal lignotuber, pseudowhorled tiers, and serrate-to-entire foliar transition.
- **`0.40–0.80` Mature Headland / Dune Tree (`4.0–25.0 m`, DBH `0.25–0.65 m`):** Reproductive specimen displaying silvery-flashing discolorous whorls, cylindrical golden-yellow flower spikes, and bivalved woody follicular cones.
- **`0.80–1.00` Senescent Gnarled Veteran (`6.0–25.0 m`, DBH `0.55–0.90 m`):** Wind-sculpted leaning patriarch with deeply fissured tessellated bark, epicormic resprouts, and persistent open woody cones.

*(Note: `ontogenetic_age_norm` is a procedural developmental stage index, not an asserted chronological age.)*

**Ecological Interactions (`RO:*`):** Winter nectar keystone supporting **New Holland Honeyeater** (*Phylidonyris novaehollandiae*), **Rainbow Lorikeet** (*Trichoglossus moluccanus*), **Eastern Spinebill** (*Acanthorhynchus tenuirostris*), and nocturnal **Sugar Glider** (*Petaurus breviceps*), while stabilizing coastal foredunes and headlands alongside *Banksia serrata* and *Banksia marginata*.

## 5. Biocultural Custodianship & Country Provenance

*Banksia integrifolia* spans coastal dunes, headlands, and estuaries across South-Eastern Australia on **Bundjalung**, **Gumbaynggirr**, **Worimi**, **Awabakal**, **Dharawal**, **Yuin**, **Gunai / Kurnai**, **Bunurong**, and **Wadawurrung** Country. Ethnographic records note soaking nectar-rich *Banksia* spikes in water for sweet seasonal drinks and carrying embers in smouldering cones. Country provenance is recorded under **Social Permissive Commons (`SPC`)** with **Local Contexts TK/BC notice placeholders** (`TK_Notice_Open_To_Collaborate`, `BC_Notice_Biocultural_Provenance`) acknowledging Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP) without asserting community-authored labels absent direct First Nations co-design.
